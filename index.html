Case study · My biggest build

Matchday

A native iPhone app for the Premier League, LaLiga, Bundesliga and Ligue 1. I designed it, built the app and its data server, stress-tested it, and cut its launch video to the beat of the music.

What it does
  • News from match data. Results with scorers, fixtures and new injuries, written by the app. No articles copied from other sites.
  • Tables in club colours. All four leagues. Follow a club and it's highlighted everywhere.
  • Form score. Every player rated 0–100 against players in the same position, with a plain-language "Why 96" and a shareable card.
  • Injuries. Who's out, who's doubtful, and when they're expected back.
  • Daily quiz. Five personal questions built from live stats, a speed bonus, a form-duel mini-game and a Today / All-time leaderboard.
  • iPhone features. Home and Lock Screen widgets, Siri and Shortcuts, offline mode, and an on-device Apple Intelligence "Catch me up" on iOS 26+.
  • Onboarding. A floodlit pitch where you flick the ball to score. Picking your club sweeps its colour across the screen.

Building with AI

AI writes the first draft. I own what ships.

Every project here was built with AI in the loop. What I bring is the framing before it and the checking after it. This is the loop I run, with evidence from my repos.

  1. Step 1

    Frame

    Before any code, I write the spec the AI has to follow. For RExchange that meant a problem spec, a data model, an AI contract and an agent rulebook, all written before the 5-hour build started.

    PROBLEM_SPEC.md · DATAMODEL.md · AICONTRACT.md · GEMINI.md
  2. Step 2

    Generate

    I let AI scaffold fast, but inside rules I set. Prompts ask for strict JSON at low temperature, and user text goes in tagged blocks so a listing can't rewrite the instructions.

    temperature: 0.1 · responseMimeType: "application/json"
  3. Step 3

    Inspect

    I read what comes back and decide where AI should not be trusted. In RExchange, AI assists and app logic controls: college boundaries are enforced in code before the model sees anything, and every response is checked against a Zod schema.

    AI never mutates state, grants access or skips validation
  4. Step 4

    Verify

    I test for the failure cases. In Matchday, a 1,000-player load test found 209 scores that never posted, so I fixed it and re-ran it until every player finished. RExchange has a 3.5-second timeout and a deterministic fallback. In my research, I traced a wrong BLS result to a harmonic of the real 1.42-day period.

    load test: 791 → 1,000 of 1,000 players · chaos tests: 0 crashes

Independent research

I measured a planet

I pulled public NASA TESS data from MAST across several sectors and built the pipeline in Python. The BLS search reported about 2.84 days. I recognised it as an integer harmonic of the true ~1.42-day period, re-folded on the literature value, and documented the issue.

Result: 2.24% depth, Rp/Rs ≈ 0.1497, radius ≈ 1.17 RJ, checked against published values. I wrote the paper myself.
